Who parks where, and why not

Eight drivers ask the campus gate for a bay. A knowledge-based system checks permits, zones, opening hours, vehicle fit, accessibility, reservations and priority, then parks the winners and tells everyone else the exact rule that stopped them.

Press Replay the morning to watch it from the driver's seat. Drag to look around, scroll to fly through the reasoning.

        How it decides

        Forward chaining starts from the facts and fires every rule whose conditions hold, cycle after cycle, until nothing new appears. Step through the ten cycles of this morning's run, or play them all.

        In the knowledge core, the 28 rules circle the facts. Each new fact flies out of the rule that derived it.

          Ask why

          Backward chaining starts from a question and works back to the facts. Pick a request: an allocation comes with its proof, a refusal with the first condition that failed.

          The proof grows as a tower beside this panel: yellow blocks are rules, green dots are stored facts. Point at a block to read it.

          This is the worked example from the report: Ali, Zara's reservation r1 and the bays, zones and values around them. Drag to turn it. Tracing a path lights up the chain of links that one rule relies on.

          Blue nodes are classes, green nodes are individuals and amber nodes are values.

          One car park, five representations

          Each formalism covers what the others cannot. They share one vocabulary, and a unit test fails if any of them uses a name the others do not.
